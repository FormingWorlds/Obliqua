Solid-Phase - fluid1d_relax

fluid1d_relax (module_fluid = "fluid1d_relax") solves the whole interior as one non-rotating, radially resolved column. Solid, mush and fluid layers, in any order, are passed through a single Henyey relaxation from the core-mantle boundary (CMB) to the surface, so tilt, loading and self-attraction of a fluid layer follow from the column solution rather than from separate terms. Without rotation the spherical-harmonic degrees decouple: only the forcing degree $n$ is solved, and the Love number is read at the surface,

\[k_n = y_5(R) - 1 .\]

Layer classes

ClassStatesMotion matrix
solid6viscoelastic $6\times6$ matrix with the complex moduli of the layer (as in Solid-Phase - solid1d-relax)
mush8poro-viscoelastic $8\times8$ matrix (as in Solid-Phase - solid1d-mush-relax)
fluid6$6\times6$ matrix with the layer's own complex shear modulus ($\mu_c \approx i\omega\eta$ for a melt), the undrained (Gassmann) bulk modulus, and Rayleigh drag

The fluid layers are the layers connected to the surface that satisfy fluid_criterion:

  • :viscosity: viscosity below visc_fluid.
  • :skeleton: the drained frame modulus is zero (failed skeleton). The drag then also acts on the solid and mush layers below, down to where the loss modulus stops exceeding the storage modulus at the forcing frequency.

Rayleigh drag acts on the horizontal motion only. In the tangential momentum row,

\[\rho\,\omega^2 \;\rightarrow\; \rho\,\omega\,(\omega - i\sigma_R).\]

With n_sub > 1 every input layer is split into n_sub sub-layers, with values interpolated between the layer centres, so the fluid interface moves continuously instead of by whole layers.

State vector

The solver orders the states so that the upper half holds displacement-like quantities and the lower half the tractions:

Slot12345678
Component$U$$V$$\Phi$$P$$X=\sigma_{rr}$$Y=\sigma_{r\theta}$$\Psi$$R$

Solid and fluid nodes carry the six slots $\{1,2,3,5,6,7\}$; mush nodes carry all eight. $P$ is the pore pressure and $R$ the radial displacement of the melt relative to the matrix.

Interfaces and core

Every face is a set of linear conditions $\pmb L\,\pmb y^- = \pmb G\,\pmb y^+$ on the face values $\pmb y^- = \pmb C_n \pmb y_n$ and $\pmb y^+ = -\pmb D_{n+1}\pmb y_{n+1}$, which gives the cell equation

\[(\pmb L \pmb C_n)\,\pmb y_n + (\pmb G \pmb D_{n+1})\,\pmb y_{n+1} = \pmb 0 .\]

slip = :noslip and pores = :closed switch the fluid faces to the alternatives. A static liquid core enters as the $4\times2$ core solution, with the CMB treated like any other interface. Above omega_sw the inertial-liquid core is used instead.

Grid and surface

Nodes sit at the cell centres of Obliqua's exponential grid (dr_min, dr_max; see Solid-Phase - solid1d-relax). The CMB rows act on the face value $\pmb y(r_C) = (\pmb I - h_1 \pmb A_1)\,\pmb y_1$ and the surface rows on $\pmb y(R) = (\pmb I + h_N \pmb A_N)\,\pmb y_N$. Gravity is evaluated at the nodes from the enclosed mass. With these choices $k_n$ converges at second order in $\Delta r$.

Heating and acceptance checks

The heating of the same solution has four channels: shear, Biot bulk, Darcy, and drag. Drag heating is

\[\dot e_d = \tfrac12\,\rho\,\sigma_R\,\omega^2\,|\pmb u_h|^2 ,\]

and in the fluid the strain is taken from the displacements, since $y_4/\mu_f$ is ill conditioned for $\mu_f \to 0$. Each solve is checked against:

  • passivity: $-\mathrm{sign}(\omega)\,\mathrm{Im}\,k_n \ge 0$;
  • the energy theorem $\frac{(2n+1)\,R\,\omega}{8\pi G}\,\bigl(-\mathrm{Im}\,k_n\bigr) = \int \dot e \, dV ,$ with a warning when the ratio departs from 1 by more than closure_tol;
  • resolution of the poroelastic boundary layers in the mush cells.

The heating profile is volume-averaged from the solver cells back onto the input layers, which conserves the integrated heating. The column model needs no load Love number, so it returns $k_L = 0$.

Function Documentation

Obliqua.run_fluid1d_relax — Function
run_fluid1d_relax(omega, rho, radius, visc, shear, bulk, bulkd, alpha, phi, perm, bulk_liq, bulk_und, R, m_core, ρ_core, μ_core, κ_core; dr_min=300, dr_max=3000, n=2, m=2, core="liquid", inertial_terms=true, visc_l=1e2, porosity_thresh=1e-5, sigma_R=1e-4, omega_sw=1e-5, visc_fluid=1e8, n_sub=1, fluid_criterion=:viscosity, slip=:freeslip, pores=:open, closure_tol=1e-2)

Use the 1D column model (fluid1d_relax.jl) to calculate kn Lovenumbers and the heating profile of the whole interior: one Henyey recursion from the CMB to the surface through solid, mush and fluid layers, the tidal Love number read at R, and the heating of the same solution (shear, bulk, Darcy and Rayleigh drag). The layers from the surface down with a viscosity below visc_fluid are fluid at every frequency (fluid1d_relax.fluid_mask); they keep their own shear modulus and get the undrained bulk modulus and the drag sigma_R.

The moduli are those at omega, of either sign (at -omega they are the conjugates, and so are k and the solution; the heating is the same). A zero-frequency mode (|omega| < 1e-18) has no heating; its k is the real part of the response at 1e-14 rad/s, the relaxed limit, with the moduli evaluated there by the caller.

Arguments

  • omega::Float64 : Forcing frequency [rad s^-1].
  • rho::Array{prec,1} : Density profile of the planet [kg m^-3].
  • radius::Array{prec,1} : Radial positions of layers, from core to surface [m].
  • visc::Array{prec,1} : Viscosity profile of the planet [Pa s].
  • shear::Array{precc,1} : Complex shear modulus profile of the planet [Pa].
  • bulk::Array{precc,1} : Complex (grain) bulk modulus profile of the planet [Pa].
  • bulkd::Array{precc,1} : Complex drained bulk modulus profile of the planet [Pa].
  • alpha::Array{precc,1} : Biot coefficient 1 - Kd/Ks.
  • phi::Array{prec,1} : Porosity profile of the planet.
  • perm::Array{prec,1} : Permeability profile of the planet [m^2].
  • bulk_liq::Array{prec,1} : Melt bulk modulus profile of the planet [Pa].
  • bulk_und::Array{prec,1} : Undrained bulk modulus profile [Pa] for the layers solved without melt variables; where it is not positive, the Gassmann modulus of bulkd, bulk, bulk_liq, phi.
  • R::prec : Planet radius [m].
  • m_core::prec : Core mass [kg].
  • ρ_core::prec : Core density [kg m^-3].
  • μ_core::precc : Core shear modulus [Pa].
  • κ_core::precc : Core bulk modulus [Pa].

Keyword Arguments

  • dr_min::Int=300 : Minimum layer thickness in m.
  • dr_max::Int=3000 : Maximum layer thickness in m.
  • n::Int=2 : Power of the radial factor (goes with (r/a)^{n}, since r<<a only n=2 contributes significantly).
  • m::Int=2 : Harmonic of the true anomaly. m=2 corresponds to the semidiurnal tide, m=1 diurnal tide.
  • core::String="liquid" : Core state; "liquid" switches to "inertial-liquid" for |omega| >= omega_sw.
  • inertial_terms::Bool=true : Inertia in the motion matrices.
  • visc_l::Float64=1e2 : Melt viscosity in the Darcy terms of the mush [Pa s].
  • porosity_thresh::Float64=1e-5 : Porosity below which a layer is non-porous.
  • sigma_R::Float64=1e-4 : Rayleigh drag rate in the fluid layers [s^-1].
  • omega_sw::Float64=1e-5 : Core switch frequency [rad s^-1].
  • visc_fluid::Float64=1e8 : Viscosity below which the layers connected to the surface are fluid [Pa s].
  • n_sub::Int=1 : Interpolated sub-layers per input layer (fluid1d_relax.refine_layers).
  • fluid_criterion::Symbol=:viscosity : :viscosity or :skeleton.
  • slip::Symbol=:freeslip : :freeslip or :noslip at solid/mush <-> fluid faces.
  • pores::Symbol=:open : :open or :closed pores at mush <-> fluid faces.
  • closure_tol::Float64=1e-2 : |Pheating / Ptide - 1| above which a warning is logged.

Returns

  • power_prf::Array{Float64,1} : Heating profile on the input layers (W m^-3 per unit forcing), volume-averaged from the solver cells.
  • Eμ_glb::Array{Float64,3} : Shear heating map (colatitude, longitude, layer).
  • Eκ_glb::Array{Float64,3} : Bulk heating map.
  • El_glb::Array{Float64,3} : Darcy heating map.
  • Ed_glb::Array{Float64,3} : Rayleigh drag heating map.
  • kn_T::ComplexF64 : Complex Tidal kn Lovenumber.
  • kn_L::ComplexF64 : Load Lovenumber; the column model needs none (0).
